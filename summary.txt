For the Polybench benchmark suite, we present the relative prediction errors across various cache configurations in \Cref{fig:prediction_heatmap}. Our predictions demonstrate strong overall accuracy, with the fully-associative (FA) configuration achieving a mean relative error of 1.51\% and a median of 0.40\% across all simulated cache sizes and 30 benchmarks. The 12-way set-associative (12WA) configuration shows comparable performance with a mean error of 1.05\% and a median of 0.14\%, indicating robust predictive capability across different cache organizations.

The use of median in addition to mean provides a more complete picture of prediction quality, as the median is less sensitive to outliers. The consistently low median values across both cache configurations (0.40\% for FA and 0.14\% for 12WA) confirm that most programs achieve highly accurate predictions. To reduce conflict misses and improve the stability of our analysis, arrays are padded to distribute memory accesses across cache sets and minimize aliasing effects.

While most benchmarks exhibit low errors, \texttt{deriche} shows a notably higher error in certain cache configurations (visible in the heatmap). This exceptional case occurs because certain cache sizes in our evaluation fall near critical turning points in the miss rate curve. At these transition regions, small variations in cache size can lead to dramatic changes in miss counts due to the sharp drop in working set coverage. A slight lag between predicted and actual turning points therefore results in disproportionately large relative errors, even when the overall trend is correctly captured.

The continuous formulation of the Smith algorithm that we developed proves particularly effective in reducing prediction errors. Unlike discrete stack distance analysis, our approach provides smooth interpolation between turning points, enabling more accurate predictions for arbitrary cache sizes. This is especially valuable in the regime between turning points where traditional methods might exhibit discontinuities or require additional simulations. The heatmap visualization clearly shows that prediction errors remain low and stable across most program-cache combinations, demonstrating the robustness of our continuous modeling approach.
